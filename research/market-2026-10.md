# Daystead market research (October 2026)

Three parallel web research passes: competitors, willingness to pay, and target customers. Caveats: several sites (apps.apple.com, revenuecat.com, adapty.io, producthunt.com) could not be opened directly from the research environment, so some figures come from search summaries. Most subscription benchmarks are published by vendors that sell paywall tools. Many cost figures are US or global, not UK.

## Verdict

1. **No verified app does bills + medication + appointments together.** Nothing found combines alarm-style dose reminders with a "money needed before payday" figure. Closest: Actually Done (bills, appointments, medical info; no dose reminders confirmed; $9.99/mo; launched July 2026).
2. **£3.99/month is not too expensive.** It sits at or below comparable apps (Medisafe $4.99, Emma and Snoop £4.99+, Famnest $5.99+). Price is not the barrier; proving the app is worth paying for when free reminders exist is.
3. **Launch with unpaid carers.** 5.8m in the UK, the only group that needs all three features at once (for someone else), the one direct competitor (Jointly) has no bills or money features, and councils already buy carer apps for residents.
4. **Paid ads will not pay back at this price.** Estimated £75 to £370 to acquire one paying user through ads, against roughly £20 to £40 earned from a subscriber who stays 6 to 12 months. Growth has to come from partnerships, communities and App Store search.
5. **Biggest revenue risk is monthly churn.** Only about 17.5% of monthly subscribers are still paying after a year (annual plans: about 44%). Lead with the annual plan.

**Threats.** (1) UK banks already do payday maths free from real bank data: [Monzo Trends](https://monzo.com/blog/balance-in-trends), [Starling Bills Manager](https://starlingbank.com/features/bills-manager). (3) Fast new entrants could add payday maths quickly. Daystead's edge: UK focus plus real medication alarms plus money planning in one place.

## Willingness to pay (benchmarks, mostly vendor data)

- Trial to paid: about 25% for trials of 4 days or less, about 42% for 17 to 32 day trials ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)).
- Hard paywall vs freemium, conversion by day 35: about 10.7% vs 2.1%, with similar retention ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)).
- 12-month retention: annual 44.1%, monthly 17.5%; Health & Fitness has the weakest first renewal at 30.3% ([appsops.store](https://appsops.store/blog/ios-subscription-churn-benchmarks-by-category-2026)).
- Health & Fitness medians: $9.70/mo, $39.99/yr; 61 to 68% of sales are annual ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)).
- Install to paying user (Health & Fitness median): 2.7% within 35 days ([Start.io citing RevenueCat](https://www.start.io/?p=101012)).
- UK subscription fatigue: 38% expect to cancel a subscription within 12 months ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/49117-svod-streaming-churn-research-2024)).
- Backlash risk: Medisafe's move to paid-only drew complaints about paying to keep reminders working (coverage mostly from a competitor, [Pillo](https://pillo.care/blog/medisafe-not-free-what-to-do)).

**Model suggested by the evidence:** a 14 to 30 day trial, annual plan shown first, and possibly basic medication reminders kept free while charging for money planning and carer/household features. This conflicts with the current no-free-tier plan in `PRODUCT_SPEC.md`; it is a decision for the owner.

**Acquisition cost.** UK cost per install roughly £2 to £10 (estimate; [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks), [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/finance-apps-apple-search-ads-aso-trends-2025/executive-summary)). At 2.7% install-to-paid, that is about £75 to £370 per paying user via ads.
